## Findings

1. **FP16 and FP32 optima are at DIFFERENT configs** (first dtype-dependent-optimum evidence with a clean same-node protocol): FP32 wants BK=16 (deep K tile, fewer SMEM round-trips); FP16 wants BK=8 + TM=16 (shallow K tile is affordable because FP16 SMEM tiles are half the bytes — it trades SMEM capacity pressure for more load rounds).
2. **At the shared config (128,128,8,16,8): FP16 33.12T > FP32 32.60T (+1.6%)** — the first dtype crossover at a competitive config. Mechanism: 2D rung is SMEM-bandwidth-bound enough that halving SMEM bytes pays; the cvt tax is amortized over TM×TN=128 FMAs.
3. FP16 reaches 98.2% of the FP32 winner's throughput (33.12 vs 33.78T).
4. **Bug fixed mid-run**: BM=BN=256 configs (512 threads × ~152 regs) exceed the 64K regs/SM limit and fail at launch — but the sweep's timing loop didn't check `cudaGetLastError`, so the GPU idled through the timed region and the event pair measured an empty queue: impossible rows (205,796 TFLOPS). Fixed by syncing + checking the error after warmup in both 1D and 2D sweeps; now correctly reported as `LAUNCH_FAIL(too many resources requested for launch)`. Lesson: **a sweep that doesn't check launch errors can "find" impossible configs** — always gate sweep rows on cudaGetLastError after warmup.

## Caveat (ABAB protocol)

Winner numbers above come from the template+dispatch build. The 1D ABAB experiment measured a 7.5% template tax for FP16 (typed hard-coded vs tuned template at the same config). If the same tax applies to 2D FP16, the hard-coded winner would be ~35.5T. Hard-coded rebuild check of (128,128,8,16,8) is the committed next step; production numbers should cite that build, not the sweep number.
